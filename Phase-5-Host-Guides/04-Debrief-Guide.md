# Debrief Guide

> The 5-minute conversation that turns a fun game into real behaviour change. Run it **right after the
> ending**, while the experience is fresh. Keep it quick, concrete, and blame-free — celebrate what they
> spotted, then connect each puzzle to work life.

**Format:** stand with the team, walk the chain in order, ask the bold question, drop the takeaway, give
the one **"at work"** action. ~30 seconds per puzzle. Total ~5 minutes (stretch to 10 with discussion).

---

## Opening line

> "Everything you just did maps to a real way organisations get breached — and a real way they get
> defended. You already did the defending. Let's name it."

---

## Puzzle-by-puzzle mapping

| # | In the game you… | Real lesson | **At work** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Caught the visitor who broke every rule | **Physical security / tailgating / visitor management** — breaches often start at the front door | Wear your badge; don't hold the door for strangers; challenge/escort visitors |
| 2 | Ignored the sticky-note password, built a passphrase | **Password security** — short, written, reused passwords fail | Use long **passphrases** + a password manager; never write them on a note |
| 3 | Entered the authenticator code | **MFA** — a stolen password isn't enough | Turn on **MFA** everywhere; treat unexpected prompts as a red flag |
| 4 | Spotted & reported the fake email | **Phishing / social engineering** — and **reporting** | Check the **sender domain**; hover links; **report**, don't just delete |
| 5 | Safely examined the planted USB | **USB / device security** — drops & baiting | **Never plug in** found USBs; hand them to IT/Security |
| 6 | Found the look-alike Wi-Fi | **Network security / rogue Wi-Fi** — evil-twin SSIDs | Verify network names; avoid sensitive work on unknown Wi-Fi; use VPN |
| 7 | Cleared Riley, exposed the contractor | **Insider threats · data classification · cloud security** | **Review third-party access**; protect 🔴 Confidential data; watch for exfil to personal cloud |
| 8 | Broke the manipulated assistant with AURORA | **AI security** — prompt injection & blind trust | **Verify** AI output; don't act on a bot's say-so for risky steps |
| 🏁 | Isolated, preserved, reported, killed it | **Incident response / reporting** — the right first moves | Know **how to report**; contain & preserve evidence; **don't pay, don't go silent, don't panic** |

> That's **all 16 awareness topics**: passwords · MFA · physical security · tailgating · visitor
> management · network security · rogue Wi-Fi · phishing · social engineering · USB security · data
> classification · cloud security · device security · incident reporting · AI security · insider threats.

---

## The headline (land this one hard)

> **"The biggest risk is the access we never review — including the people we trust."**
>
> You instinctively suspected the angry ex-employee. The evidence said otherwise: it was the **trusted
> contractor** with a badge nobody re-checked. Real insider incidents look exactly like that."

---

## Discussion questions (pick 2–3 if time allows)

1. Which red flag would you have **missed** in a busy real workday — and what would help you catch it?
2. You find a USB in the car park. **Walk me through** what you actually do.
3. An email says your password expires in 1 hour. What are the **three things** you check first?
4. Who would you call **right now** if you suspected a breach at work? (If they don't know — that's the
   most valuable takeaway of the day.)
5. What's one access right or account **we should review** that probably hasn't been looked at in a while?

---

## Closing (30 seconds)

> "You didn't need to be technical to stop this — you needed to **slow down, verify, and report**. That's
> security. Take three habits back to your desk: **passphrase + MFA**, **pause-and-check before you
> click**, and **report anything odd early**. Thanks for being NIMBUS's IR team today."

[Optional: hand out a takeaway card, point them to your real internal "how to report" channel, or invite
them to the next session. End on the win.]

---

## Tailoring tips
- **Short on time?** Do P1, P4, P7, Finale only — they carry the strongest behaviours.
- **Technical audience?** Go deeper on evil-twin Wi-Fi, prompt injection, and cloud exfil detection.
- **Leadership audience?** Emphasise the headline: third-party access reviews and classification.
- Always swap in your organisation's **real reporting contact** at the end.
