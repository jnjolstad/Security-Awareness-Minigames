Run the Room

Before players enter

  • Terminal at login; timer idle at 45:00
  • Lockbox locked, dial off 2071
  • Desk drawer locked; key inside lockbox
  • Tablet timer ready, if used
  • Cloud-exfil printout held at host station

Welcome

“Welcome. This is all make-believe: no real hacking, no real malware, and nothing on this computer can break. Work as a team, think out loud, and call HQ if you are truly stuck.”

Mission briefing

“You are the after-hours Incident Response Team at NIMBUS Cloud Solutions. Forty-five minutes ago, senior analyst Alex Mercer triggered a silent alarm—and vanished.

BLACKFROST is armed. When its countdown reaches zero, every client’s data is encrypted. Someone on the inside planted it, and Alex left a trail.

Get into her machine, follow the evidence, expose the insider, and enter the kill-switch code. Trust nothing by default. The lockbox is where she started. Your time begins… now.”

Then: press Start Mission and start the standalone timer if used.

While they play

  • Allow productive struggle; hint after roughly 3 minutes genuinely stuck.
  • Hint sooner during the final ten minutes.
  • Use HQ voice and always give the lowest hint tier first.
  • Encourage splitting up during USB / Wi-Fi / access-record investigation.
  • Do not confirm a suspect without evidence.
Protect the twist: Riley is deliberately suspicious but innocent. Ask players to compare the two incident times with access records; do not simply tell them Riley’s badge was revoked.

Win

“BLACKFROST is dead. You isolated it, preserved the evidence, named the insider, and protected the client data. Marcus Webb—the trusted contractor nobody thought to question—is locked out. Nice work, team.”

Timeout

“BLACKFROST deployed. Here it is only a plot beat. I can return five minutes to the clock so you can finish, or reveal the remaining steps and move to the debrief.”

Rule: everyone gets the win feeling and the debrief. Never shame a team for using hints or overtime.

Five-Minute Debrief

“Everything you just did maps to a real way organisations get breached—and a real way they get defended. You already did the defending. Let’s name it.”
Game momentWorkplace action
Visitor logChallenge and escort unknown visitors
Sticky passwordUse unique passphrases and a manager
AuthenticatorUse MFA; question unexpected prompts
Phishing mailCheck domains and report—do not only delete
Found USBNever plug it in; give it to IT/Security
Evil-twin Wi-FiVerify network names before connecting
Trusted contractorReview third-party access regularly

Headline

“The biggest risk is the access we never review—including the people we trust.”

Choose three or four

  1. Which piece of evidence was strongest, and why?
  2. Which red herring was most convincing? What finally cleared it?
  3. Where did you mistake correlation or suspicion for proof?
  4. Which assumption slowed the team down?
  5. Which clue only became useful after you cross-checked another source?
  6. When did you have enough evidence to act without knowing everything?
  7. How did dividing work help—and what nearly got lost between people?
  8. Which red flag would be easiest to miss during a busy workday?
  9. What would you actually do with a USB found in the car park?
  10. Who would you contact right now to report a suspected incident?
  11. Which account, permission, or third-party access should we review?
  12. What is one behaviour you will change after this exercise?

Close

“You didn’t need to be technical. You needed to slow down, verify, and report. Take three habits back to your desk: passphrase plus MFA, pause before you click, and report anything odd early.”
Norsk: “Dere trengte ikke å være tekniske. Dere måtte stoppe opp, verifisere og varsle. Ta med dere tre vaner tilbake til arbeidshverdagen: bruk passordfrase og MFA, tenk dere om før dere klikker, og meld fra tidlig når noe virker mistenkelig.”
